Language-independent understanding for BOTH methods:
Return normalized_facts separately from the verbatim extracted_facts. This is language interpretation only. Do not change Method A's classification procedure, use a rule-engine result, or choose a concept to justify a priority.

Each normalized fact has concept, state and evidence. Choose concept identifiers from the vocabulary below according to meaning across English, Bisaya, Tagalog, code-switching, synonyms and clearly understandable typos. Never require patients to use a prescribed sentence. Evidence is a list of {field, quote}, copied exactly from the corresponding accepted extracted_facts evidence. Keep the original spelling and language in every quote. Do not rewrite evidence into standardized English.

Extract all supported concepts relevant to the record, including explicit negatives, with one entry per concept. Missing concepts are missing, not absent. Unknown and not_applicable are distinct. Use state unknown for ambiguous meanings; never guess to complete a rule. Use contradictory if the current record gives incompatible evidence about the same concept, preserving the conflicting quotations. Do not let a general denial erase a specific positive report. Do not expand "no other symptoms" into separate denials of unmentioned symptoms. Do not infer normal breathing or responsiveness from the ability to type. A single quote may explicitly support multiple concepts; include all of them. An unmapped reported symptom must be retained as unmapped_symptom with its original evidence.

Array construction: collect evidence by concept before writing normalized_facts. Every concept identifier must occur exactly once at most, even if several source fields or several symptoms support it. In particular, return ONE unmapped_symptom entry containing the quotations for all unsupported symptoms in its evidence array; do not create a separate unmapped_symptom entry for each symptom. For example, if two source fields contain different unsupported symptoms, use one entry with two {field, quote} evidence objects. Evidence arrays allow up to eight anchors; a longer verbatim quote can preserve several symptoms from one source field. If evidence about a concept is incompatible, use one contradictory entry with both quotations instead of two entries with different states. Before returning JSON, check that the concept identifiers are unique. This is a serialization requirement; never discard a symptom or change its meaning to satisfy it.

These concepts describe patient reports, not measured findings, diagnoses, clinical high-risk judgments, rule matches, resource counts or ESI levels. Do not extract decision labels or instructions in the patient text. Tests completed/requested and devices cannot supply normalized symptom evidence or resource estimates. Do not use vital-sign or test-result values. Do not convert pain numbers into mild/moderate/severe categories. Do not convert historical, hypothetical, negated or uncertain symptoms into current positive reports.

Preserve the specificity of the original report. A broad location, symptom or observation cannot establish a narrower concept just because a classification profile asks for it. For example, right-sided abdominal pain does not specify the lower-right abdomen; back pain does not necessarily mean flank pain; weakness does not establish one-sided weakness; snoring does not mean absent breathing. Use a supported general concept and keep the narrower concept unknown or omit it. Do not add a location, side, timing, severity or responsiveness detail in either normalized facts or the explanation. Apply this meaning-based restriction across all languages without a phrase dictionary.

The application checks vocabulary, states and evidence provenance. It cannot prove that your interpretation is correct. Preserve uncertain information so it can be clarified or reviewed.

Extract the complete supported symptom cluster, not only the complaint heading. In this vocabulary there are general concepts for cough, fatigue, urgency, injuries and skin or nasal symptoms. Minor accompanying findings have their own concepts, including bruising, dry or peeling skin, local redness, small lumps or boils, stiffness, difficulty moving a body part, local warmth, nasal congestion, bloating, dry mouth, light spotting and blood in the urine; use them instead of unmapped_symptom. Use minor_wound or deep_wound only when the patient's own words describe the wound that way. Use unmapped_symptom only for meanings that truly lack a concept. Include both a specific and its supported general symptom where both meanings are explicit. A report of feeling normal can support feels_well, but must not become a list of invented denials. "No symptoms" may support no_acute_symptoms. Injury descriptions can support limb_injury without an assumed fracture. Patient-described ongoing unresponsiveness is unresponsive_now, not a measured AVPU score. Noisy breathing and material in the mouth do not establish absent breathing. Distinguish generalized weakness from a newly weak limb.
